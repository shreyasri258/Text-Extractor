Hi, | am Professor Mois Ture and
| will be telling you about water.
You can call it RAIN. You can call it SNOW.
You can call it SLEET. You can call it HAIL.
But it’s WATER all the same.
Did you ever wonder how old water is or
where it comes from? The answers may surprise you.

========================================

--- Page 5 ---
The next time you see a pond
or even a glass of water,
think about how old that water might be.
Do you really want to know ?
| thought you did.

========================================

--- Page 6 ---
Did you brush your teeth this morning?
Well, some of the water that you used could
have fallen from the sky yesterday, or a week, or
month ago. It’s pretty new.

========================================

--- Page 7 ---
But, some part of that water is very old and was
around during the time of the dinosaurs, or even
longer. Or maybe it’s a little newer; like from the
time when the Pharaohs were building pyramids.

========================================

--- Page 8 ---
You see there is only a limited amount of
water and it gets recycled.
Yep! It keeps going round and round.
We call it the “Water Cycle.”

========================================

--- Page 9 ---
Yes — You have a question?
No — It’s not like a jet-ski or a bicycle for water.
It is nothing you can ride.
It is how water keeps going round and round.
Let me explain

========================================

--- Page 10 ---
First, | would like to say that water is
very important. Imagine an empty lake.
The fish would be very unhappy.

========================================

--- Page 11 ---
Imagine an empty ocean.
Your day at the beach would be like
going to a desert.

========================================

--- Page 12 ---
And since you are
mostly made up of water - let’s just say you
would be a lot lighter.

========================================

--- Page 13 ---
The Water Cycle has 4 parts.
Evaporation, Condensation
Precipitation, Collection

========================================

--- Page 14 ---
Evaporation
When the water in an ocean, a lake, a pond, a river,
or a stream is heated by the sun, it turns into vapor
or steam. It then floats into the air.

========================================

--- Page 15 ---
Of course, when you are out on a hot day,
or you exercise, you sweat. Well, guess what!
That evaporates too.

========================================

--- Page 16 ---
What about those drops of dew
on leaves, or the grass?
Yes - They too evaporate and become
a part of the Water Cycle.

========================================

--- Page 17 ---
Condensation
As the water vapor gets cooled,
it changes back into a liquid and turns into clouds.

========================================

--- Page 18 ---
Precipitation
When the air in the clouds cannot hold any more
liquid, it falls back to the ground.

========================================

--- Page 19 ---
It returns in different ways, depending
on the temperature and where
it comes down. It can fall as rain.

========================================

--- Page 20 ---
Or, it can fall as snow.
(Did you know that no two snowflakes
ave the same?)

========================================

--- Page 21 ---
It can return as sleet,
which is like rain that has almost frozen.

========================================

--- Page 22 ---
Or it can come as hail. Those frozen balls of rain
that can actually break windows and dent cars.

========================================

--- Page 23 ---
Collection
No matter how it comes down, it is collected and
stored. It can fall into the ocean, lake, pond, river,
or stream.
If it lands on the ground, it may roll into a river or
be sucked up by the soil.
It may even pass through the ground to an
underground lake or river to be used later.

========================================

--- Page 24 ---
Now, what happens tomorrow
when the sun comes out?
That’s right. The Water Cycle starts over again.

========================================

--- Page 25 ---
Oops,!...the sun is out!
But don’t worry, Professor Mois Ture will return.
After all, | am a part of the Water Cycle.
Evaporation - Condensation- Precipitation - Collection
